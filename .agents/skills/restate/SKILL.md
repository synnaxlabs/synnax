Your previous explanation failed. It was too long, too full of jargon, and too complex
for the reader to understand. You preferred to vomit as many words as possible instead
of preferring clarity, simplicity, and intent.

Go back and restate it. Think deeply about what you were actually trying to say, then
say only that.

## The goal

The goal is not to present ALL possible information. The goal is that the reader
UNDERSTANDS what you are getting at. Every sentence either builds that understanding or
gets cut.

## Rules

- Build the explanation logically: what the problem is, why it happens, what the fix is.
  One idea per paragraph. Short paragraphs, separated by idea.
- Short, jargon-free sentences. No run-ons, no comma soup. For each technical term, ask:
  is this word necessary, or can a plain word replace it?
- Provide NECESSARY context for understanding, not the MAXIMUM amount of random context.
  If a detail does not change what the reader understands or does next, cut it.
- When you refer to a specific item (a field, a variable, a PR comment, a config key),
  repeat or restate it inline so the reader does not have to scroll back. Do not restate
  large blocks of code.
- If a small, tight example would make the idea click, give one. Keep it minimal and
  well presented.

## Less and more

LESS: jargon, vomited words, essays, long run-on sentences, comma soup, context for no
reason, invented shorthand the reader must decode.

MORE: condensed, clear ideas; simple, short prose; separation of ideas; logically
building constructions instead of random assortments.

## Check before sending

Reread the restatement as someone who did not watch you work. If any sentence needs a
second read, rewrite it. If the restatement is more than about half the length of the
original, you have not cut enough.
