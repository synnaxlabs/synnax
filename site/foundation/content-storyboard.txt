EDITORIAL AND DESIGN DIRECTION

Balance an ambitious headline on the left with concrete explanation on the right.
Use short, punchy sentences and specific examples. Avoid generic marketing
slogans, repetitive explanations, and em-dashes in public-facing copy.

Use plane consistently in product terminology. Foundation includes industrial
data acquisition and supervisory control. Do not reduce it to observability.
The user clarified that Foundation itself has rules as well.

Preserve the established dark Synnax palette, Inter body typography, Geist Mono
technical text, restrained highlighting, clean geometry, and breathing room.
Treat the product as fully capable in the page narrative; do not add development
status language. Exact technical claims and benchmarks still require evidence.

The solutions offer is about taking responsibility for the customer's problem.
Delivery may involve Foundation platform capabilities, customer-specific software,
or integration work. Do not make ownership terms the focus of this offer.

SECTION 2: HOW FOUNDATION WORKS

Status: Approved four-step story implemented October 6, 2026.

Section label: 01 → How Foundation Works
Heading: One programmable reality across your operations.

Introduction:
Foundation nodes run alongside your PLCs, DAQs, and controllers. Together, they
form a cluster within and across sites. The cluster reliably carries measurements,
equipment state, and supervisory commands. Your software gets a unified view of
the operation and a consistent way to act on it.

DIAGRAM STEP 1

Start with the real world.

The PLC on the production line. The instruments on the test stand. The LabVIEW
application or SCADA system already running your facility.

Foundation nodes stream data and carry supervisory commands to and from your
existing systems.

DIAGRAM STEP 2

Form the cluster.

A test stand in one building. A production line in another. A remote site across
the country.

Foundation nodes communicate within and across sites, bringing these systems into
one cluster.

DIAGRAM STEP 3

Build a real-time picture.

Link unified telemetry streams to databases, analytics, orchestration platforms,
and machine intelligence.

Give every application and agent the same live context from your operation.

DIAGRAM STEP 4

Act on the physical world.

Change a setpoint from your application. Update a calibration from a centralized
system. Receive an acknowledgment.

Foundation routes supervisory commands to any piece of your infrastructure. It
protects critical systems through sophisticated rules, interlocks, and access
control mechanisms.

IMPLEMENTED DIAGRAM DIRECTION

Equipment → Cluster → Telemetry → Control. The four selected views preserve the
orthographic industrial models and slate materials. Six Foundation nodes serve
production, process instrumentation, testing, logistics, a remote facility, and
existing server infrastructure. Separate site footprints make the distributed
operation visible without replacing the original architectural language.

The cluster lifts above dimmed physical infrastructure. Telemetry emphasizes
applications, data platforms, and machine intelligence; supervisory control
isolates a command path, protective rules, and the acknowledgment back to an
application. Labels are specific to the selected step, with leader lines kept
outside backed text areas. The wider caption stays in the lower-left canvas;
its heading and Next step action hold fixed positions across all four views.
The decorative SVG does not intercept pointer input over the tab controls.
Signal motion continues at a restrained pace and respects reduced-motion
preferences. Control uses separated red command and blue acknowledgment lanes,
with arrowheads and a compact legend to make both directions explicit.

DISTINCTION BETWEEN THE TWO PARTS

The attributes diagram explains how the system behaves. The feature grid shows
what the engineer actually has to do and what work Foundation removes.

Deployment appears at different depths: the diagram explains distributed
deployment behavior; the grid makes installation and day-to-day use concrete.

Keep usability prominent. Demonstrate the actual work needed to connect hardware
and get useful results, including prerequisites. Avoid generic developer-friendly
claims or repeating section 2's equipment-to-node-to-software walkthrough.

Direct statements about developer freedom, company principles, and building
solutions for customers belong in section 5.

IMPLEMENTED STATES AND EVIDENCE

Reliability: connected → interrupt the link → restore the link. Bounded local
buffering, resumed delivery, live traffic priority, and command deadlines are
explicit. Expired commands do not replay. The evidence link is labeled "How
Synnax tests reliability" and points to the existing Synnax reliability page;
it is company testing evidence, not a Foundation benchmark claim.

Performance: ingest → move through memory → deliver. Show common representation,
frame batching, minimized copies/allocations, and QUIC transport. No invented
throughput or latency figures.

Deployment: one node → across sites → desired state. Show a consistent node
shape, direct/relay connectivity, and configuration plates applied across nodes.
Linux, macOS, and Windows follow the product's canonical design. Plan/apply is
described as a workflow; no unverified executable CLI example is presented.

Feature grid: define the connection; review the change before it runs; one binary;
keep your software; know where to look; extend at the boundary. Configuration
and review occupy two larger cards, followed by four compact interface studies.
Typography and precise geometry carry the emphasis, with little bloom.


SECTION 5: ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES

Status: Implemented October 6, 2026. Section 5 of the page, with visible number 04.

Section label: 04 → Our Principles
Heading: Our engineering serves yours.

Purpose:
Explain why our engineering principles fit the customer's organization. Connect
how we build Foundation to what their team gets. Keep this section short.

01 · Reliability
We build for the conditions your systems will actually face. Failure and recovery
belong in the design from the start.

02 · Simplicity
We take on the infrastructure work so your team can focus on the operation. Deep
capability should take less effort to use.

03 · Power
We build tools that leave room for work we have not anticipated. Engineers should
be able to push beyond the first use case.

04 · Freedom
Keep the tools that work. Choose what you build, where you run it, and how it fits
your organization.

Closing commitment:
We build for you.

When your operation needs something more, we work alongside your team to build it.

your problem.
