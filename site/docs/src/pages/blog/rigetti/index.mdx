Rigetti Computing builds and operates superconducting quantum computers at near absolute
zero temperatures.

Reliably running experiments on novel processors requires precise control, warmup, and
cooldown of quantum dilution refrigerators.

Rigetti is adopting Synnax to simplify and accelerate the process of operating these
fridges. Together, we're optimizing automated warmup and cooldown procedures, simulating
critical workflows before deployment, and building tools to improve operational
visibility.

All of this serves one objective: faster turnaround times to run more experiments on the
latest quantum technologies.

## Why warmup and cooldown matter

Dilution refrigerators keep quantum processors operating at a fraction of a degree above
absolute zero. To change a processor, operators warm the fridge to room temperature,
make the swap, and then cool it back down. Both warmup and cooldown require long,
complex procedures of pump, valve, heater, and gas-handling steps. Pressure,
temperature, and other critical variables need to be carefully monitored throughout
these processes.

Experiments cannot be run on a fridge while it is cooling down or warming up. Every hour
of efficiency extracted from these processes means more time for engineers to run
critical tests on chips.
